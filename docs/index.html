Settler Quote Engine

Two problems. 1. A firm quote is a free option: price the time it stays open. 2. Finite inventory under many live quotes: how many to keep out. Everything below is computed live; the dots are the engine's quote rule run against the specified market.

1 · What a firm quote gives away

Markup versus award window

Break-even markup m*(W): gas floor + the expected loss to originators who exercise against us + carry. Returns are Student-t with ν degrees of freedom (variance σ²W); drag ν to the right for the Gaussian limit the spec simulates. The spec's rate is a random walk; for a pinned corridor set a mean-reversion half-life and the move over the window saturates, so the price of time stops growing.

The call you wrote

The standard option chart. Underlying: the rate at expiry (today = 1.000). Strike: your price. One line: your P&L as the writer of the call. Left of the strike the originator walks and you make nothing; right of it he exercises and you pay the difference on the destination units you promised. A textbook writer starts at +premium; you start at zero, because the quote is firm and free. The premium is collected through the markup instead: that is the whole pricing problem.

2 · Finite inventory, many live quotes

Each live quote reserves one payment. With capacity C and n live quotes, the awards A are the uninformed (they always fill) plus the informed, who watch the same rate path and fill together with probability q or not at all. The n-th quote adds ΔEn = q·1{n > C} + (1−p)(1−q)·P(Bin(n−1, 1−p) ≥ C) expected unfunded awards, non-decreasing in n, so expected profit n·p_fill·margin − F·E[(A − C)⁺] is concave and the optimum is a critical fractile: keep quoting while p_fill·margin ≥ F·ΔEn. F = ∞ is hard reservation (n = C).

Why the window costs inventory, not price

Hard reservation, capacity 10, spec market: fills per second from the engine against the renewal closed form C·p_fill / (held + 1/λ). Longer windows keep slots reserved but idle.
