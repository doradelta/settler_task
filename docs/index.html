Settler Quote Engine

Two problems. 1. A firm quote is a free option: price the time it stays open. 2. Finite capital and payments of different sizes: which auctions to quote. Everything below is computed live; the dots are the engine's quote rule run against the specified market.

1 · What a firm quote gives away

Markup versus award window

Break-even markup m*(W): gas floor + the expected loss to originators who exercise against us + carry. Returns are Student-t with ν degrees of freedom (variance σ²W); drag ν to the right for the Gaussian limit the spec simulates. The spec's rate is a random walk; for a pinned corridor set a mean-reversion half-life and the move over the window saturates, so the price of time stops growing.

What one quote pays

Covered-call style: each leg, then the package. Underlying on the x-axis: the rate at expiry (today = 1.000); strike: your price. Orange, the informed (30 %): a call you wrote for free. Below the strike he walks and you make nothing; above it he exercises, you pay the difference plus gas. Green, the uninformed (70 %): he fills whatever the rate, so you are short a forward with the markup as cushion: you keep markup − gas if the rate stayed, more if it fell, less if it rose. Blue, the package: 0.7 × green + 0.3 × orange. The markup lifts the green and blue lines and pushes the strike right; the fair markup is where the blue line, weighted by where the rate actually lands, averages to zero.

2 · Finite capital, payments of different sizes

With one payment size there is nothing to decide: quote first-come up to capacity, never naked. The decision appears when sizes vary. Gas is a fixed cost per fill, so a quote of size a earns α·a − β: small payments lose money, and every quote ties up a·T of capital-time. Which auctions to quote is a fractional knapsack: maximise Σ x·(α·a − β) subject to Σ x·a·T ≤ C. Its solution is greedy by yield (profit per unit of capital per second), which rises with size, so the optimal policy is a threshold: quote iff a ≥ a_min. The LP's dual λ* is the bid price: what every unit of capital must earn per hour. Capital slack: λ* = 0 and a_min is the gas break-even size. Capital scarce: a_min rises until demand just fills the capital.
